🧠 What is Thiamine (B1)?

Thiamine is the essential "key" that allows your body to turn food into energy. Every single cell requires it, but the brain is the most sensitive to its absence.

Unlike other vitamins, the body cannot produce its own Thiamine and can only store a small amount (about 2-3 weeks' worth).

🚨 Why B1 Deficiency is So Devastating

The brain runs on glucose. But glucose cannot enter brain cells without Thiamine (B1)—it's the literal key that unlocks the door.

Without enough B1, brain cells begin to starve and die within weeks. Unlike other organs, the brain cannot survive long without its fuel supply. This isn't "weakness" or "aging"—it's cell death.

Once brain cells die, they do not grow back. This is why untreated Wernicke's leaves permanent, irreversible damage.

⚠️ The Early Warning Signs:

  • Extreme Fatigue: Profound exhaustion that sleep doesn't fix.
  • Angry / Irritability: Feeling unusually short-tempered, "on edge," or unexplainably angry.
  • Paranoia: Intense, irrational suspicion or distrust of others (if present).
  • Muscle Weakness: Feeling heavy or "rubbery" in the limbs.
  • Brain Fog: Difficulty following conversations or making decisions.

👨‍⚕️ How is it Diagnosed?

The Clinical Equation
Ataxia + Ocular Issues + Confusion + Alcohol = Wernicke's

Mum has all of these.

WKS is a clinical diagnosis. It is diagnosed based on the "Classic Triad" above combined with a risk factor.

The Sensitivity Problem (MRI & Labs)

An MRI is not sensitive enough to detect Wernicke's in its early, reversible stage. Studies show that MRI only catches about 26% to 53% of cases.

Why isn't a Blood Test used?
Standard thiamine blood tests take days or weeks to process—time that a brain in crisis does not have. Furthermore, blood levels often don't reflect the levels in the brain, leading to "false normals."

Essentially: A "normal" MRI or blood test does not rule out WKS. If the symptoms are there, treatment must start immediately.

The Diagnosis Formula Doctors are trained to look for the Classic Triad:
  • 1. Confusion / Mental State Change
  • 2. Ataxia (Balance/Coordination issues)
  • 3. Ocular (Eye) Abnormalities
If a person has any of these plus a Risk Factor (like chronic alcohol use), medical guidelines state they should be treated for B1 deficiency immediately.

📉 Korsakoff Syndrome: The Permanent Stage

Korsakoff Syndrome is the chronic stage of Wernicke's Encephalopathy. It occurs when the initial "brain starvation" is left untreated, resulting in irreversible damage to the memory centers of the brain.

1. Severe Short-Term Amnesia: The brain can no longer "record" what happened five minutes ago. You can have a full conversation, and five minutes later, she has no memory of it and the conversation starts over from the beginning.

👁️ Hallucinations & The "Matchbox Sign"

Auditory & Visual Hallucinations: Hearing voices that are not there or seeing objects, insects, or organisms that do not exist.

The "Matchbox Sign": This is a documented medical phenomenon where a patient is so convinced they are seeing or feeling small organisms (like worms or insects) that they collect debris—like lint or dust—in a small container to show the doctor as "proof."

💊 The Treatment Protocol

Because the brain is in an acute state of starvation, the treatment must be aggressive and immediate. Standard vitamin tablets are often not enough because the gut's ability to absorb thiamine is severely compromised in chronic alcohol users.

Pabrinex (Parenteral Thiamine)

The gold standard treatment is Pabrinex—high-dose thiamine administered directly into the bloodstream (IV) or muscle (IM). This is known as Parenteral administration. It bypasses the damaged gut and forces the nutrient into the brain to stop further cell death.

The "Before Glucose" Rule

Crucially, high-dose B1 must be administered before the patient is given any sugary foods or glucose drips. Giving glucose to a thiamine-deficient person can trigger a sudden, catastrophic worsening of the condition.

Clinical Goal: To flood the brain with thiamine while it is still in the "reversible" Wernicke's stage, preventing the permanent and irreversible damage of Korsakoff Syndrome.
